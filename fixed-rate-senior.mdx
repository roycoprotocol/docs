---
title: "Fixed-Rate Senior Tranche"
description: "How the fixed Senior rate works and how it affects Junior and the Liquidity Provider Tranche."
---

Royco Day markets can use a fixed rate to calculate a payment for the Senior tranche, called a **coupon**. The rate determines how the coupon is calculated, but does not guarantee payment. Payment depends on market gains and available Junior coverage.

## How each tranche works

- **Senior (ST):** has a coupon calculated using the market's fixed rate. Deposits and withdrawals still follow the market's coverage, liquidity, and redemption rules.
- **Junior (JT):** absorbs losses before Senior and may cover a coupon shortfall. If Junior covers a loss or shortfall, future gains first go toward restoring that amount. Junior receives yield left after the coupon and LPT premium.
- **Liquidity Provider Tranche (LPT):** provides secondary liquidity for Senior shares. It may receive a liquidity premium from gains left after the Senior coupon. The market's liquidity settings determine the premium.

## When the coupon is calculated

The coupon builds over time. The market settles it when an update records a change in collateral value. If the recorded value does not change, the coupon is not settled and its calculation period stays open.

The amount is based on the fixed rate, the time since the last settlement, and the Senior value recorded by the market.

## How gains and losses are handled

When the market records a loss, Junior absorbs it first. Losses beyond the Junior coverage affect Senior. If Junior covers a coupon shortfall, that amount is recorded as a Junior loss and future gains first go toward restoring it. Any coupon the market cannot pay is cleared at that update and does not carry forward.

When the market records a gain, it first restores recorded Junior losses, then pays the Senior coupon. If the gain is not enough, the remaining Junior coverage may cover part of the coupon. Any gain left after that can pay the LPT premium, and Junior receives the rest.

The LPT premium is paid in Senior shares, so it does not increase Senior exposure to the market.

## Recovery period

A market may enter a temporary recovery period after Junior absorbs a loss, if it has not reached its liquidation threshold. This period is called **Fixed Term** in the market settings.

During the recovery period, Senior and Junior deposits and withdrawals pause. LPT withdrawals also pause, but LPT deposits remain available. Gains first restore recorded Junior losses, so the regular LPT premium and related excess-yield fees pause. A fee on a Senior coupon that is paid may still apply.

The market returns to normal when the Junior loss is restored. It also returns to normal if the recovery period ends, the market reaches its liquidation threshold, or the Junior buffer is exhausted. If any Junior loss remains at that point, it is cleared. Markets with no Fixed Term duration do not enter this recovery period.

## Market settings

Each market sets the Senior rate, the model and cap used to calculate the LPT premium, and an administrator who can update these settings. A rate change applies to the coupon period already in progress, so it is best made just after a coupon is settled.

In a fixed-rate market, the Senior coupon takes the place of the variable risk premium used in other Day markets. The LPT premium still depends on the market's liquidity settings, and Junior receives any yield left over.
